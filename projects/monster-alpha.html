The mechanic

The player auto-attacks while kiting waves of monsters, leveling up to choose weapon, passive, or transformation upgrades. The one deliberate strategic layer: a Monster King transformation, fuelled by energy that fills passively, activated by choice — burn it early for safety, or bank it for the scripted final wave.

A pivot in the upgrade design

Weapons carry rarity → strongest weapons gated behind luck Rarity moved to per-weapon upgrades instead

With a small prototype weapon pool, weapon-level rarity meant players could see the same two or three weapons repeatedly, railroading builds. Moving rarity onto the upgrades kept every weapon reachable while still giving anticipation at each level-up — and opened the door to weapon evolutions, which testers responded to best.

Tuning against what testers did

An issue-hunting pass flagged that the healing pickup's drop rate let players survive mid-game without moving — undercutting the kiting skill the loop is meant to test. Recommendation: lower the drop rate specifically in the mid-game window, where it was doing the most damage to the intended challenge.

What I'd do differently

Passives never got integrated with the transformation system — they stayed flat, linear stat sticks while everything else escalated. Next time, passives would hook directly into the transformation state.
